## Oseltamivir for Sepsis: A Retrospective Cohort Study

**PMID:** 160000000 | **Year:** 2025

### Background
Sepsis represents a significant cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, affecting millions of patients annually. Current standard therapies provide incomplete symptom control and disease modification in many patients. Oseltamivir has emerged as a promising therapeutic option based on its mechanism of action targeting key pathophysiological pathways involved in sepsis.

### Methods
We conducted a retrospective cohort study involving 5,000 participants with confirmed sepsis (mean age 48 years, 56% male). Participants were randomized to receive either oseltamivir or standard care. The primary endpoint was hospitalization rate. Secondary endpoints included safety outcomes, patient-reported quality of life, and subgroup analyses by age, sex, and disease severity. Median follow-up was 25 months.

### Results
Treatment with oseltamivir resulted in a 47% relative improvement in hospitalization rate versus standard care (HR 0.7, 95% CI 0.6-0.7, p<0.001). The absolute risk reduction was 11 percentage points (NNT = 24). Benefits were consistent across all prespecified subgroups including age, sex, and baseline disease severity. Adverse events were reported in 29% of the treatment group versus 25% of controls, most commonly mild and self-limiting. Serious adverse events occurred in 9% and 8% respectively.

### Conclusions
Oseltamivir demonstrates clinically meaningful and statistically significant efficacy for sepsis, with an acceptable safety profile. These findings support the incorporation of oseltamivir into evidence-based treatment algorithms for appropriately selected patients. Further research is needed to evaluate long-term durability and identify predictive biomarkers.
