## Infliximab for Peptic Ulcer Disease: A Multicenter RCT

**PMID:** 160000000 | **Year:** 2020

### Background
Peptic Ulcer Disease represents a significant cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, affecting millions of patients annually. Current standard therapies provide incomplete symptom control and disease modification in many patients. Infliximab has emerged as a promising therapeutic option based on its mechanism of action targeting key pathophysiological pathways involved in peptic ulcer disease.

### Methods
We conducted a multicenter rct involving 5,000 participants with confirmed peptic ulcer disease (mean age 60 years, 57% male). Participants were randomized to receive either infliximab or standard care. The primary endpoint was quality of life. Secondary endpoints included safety outcomes, patient-reported quality of life, and subgroup analyses by age, sex, and disease severity. Median follow-up was 12 months.

### Results
Treatment with infliximab resulted in a 36% relative improvement in quality of life versus standard care (HR 0.6, 95% CI 0.78-0.88, p<0.001). The absolute risk reduction was 9 percentage points (NNT = 15). Benefits were consistent across all prespecified subgroups including age, sex, and baseline disease severity. Adverse events were reported in 34% of the treatment group versus 31% of controls, most commonly mild and self-limiting. Serious adverse events occurred in 10% and 7% respectively.

### Conclusions
Infliximab demonstrates clinically meaningful and statistically significant efficacy for peptic ulcer disease, with an acceptable safety profile. These findings support the incorporation of infliximab into evidence-based treatment algorithms for appropriately selected patients. Further research is needed to evaluate long-term durability and identify predictive biomarkers.
