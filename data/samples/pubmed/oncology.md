## Trastuzumab for Breast Cancer: A Multicenter RCT

**PMID:** 160000000 | **Year:** 2024

### Background
Breast Cancer represents a significant cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, affecting millions of patients annually. Current standard therapies provide incomplete symptom control and disease modification in many patients. Trastuzumab has emerged as a promising therapeutic option based on its mechanism of action targeting key pathophysiological pathways involved in breast cancer.

### Methods
We conducted a multicenter rct involving 28,000 participants with confirmed breast cancer (mean age 72 years, 45% male). Participants were randomized to receive either trastuzumab or standard care. The primary endpoint was symptom severity. Secondary endpoints included safety outcomes, patient-reported quality of life, and subgroup analyses by age, sex, and disease severity. Median follow-up was 13 months.

### Results
Treatment with trastuzumab resulted in a 22% relative improvement in symptom severity versus standard care (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.46-0.72, p<0.001). The absolute risk reduction was 24 percentage points (NNT = 30). Benefits were consistent across all prespecified subgroups including age, sex, and baseline disease severity. Adverse events were reported in 21% of the treatment group versus 12% of controls, most commonly mild and self-limiting. Serious adverse events occurred in 9% and 9% respectively.

### Conclusions
Trastuzumab demonstrates clinically meaningful and statistically significant efficacy for breast cancer, with an acceptable safety profile. These findings support the incorporation of trastuzumab into evidence-based treatment algorithms for appropriately selected patients. Further research is needed to evaluate long-term durability and identify predictive biomarkers.
