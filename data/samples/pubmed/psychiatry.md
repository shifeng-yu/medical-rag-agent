## Brexpiprazole for Bipolar Disorder: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

**PMID:** 160000000 | **Year:** 2020

### Background
Bipolar Disorder represents a significant cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, affecting millions of patients annually. Current standard therapies provide incomplete symptom control and disease modification in many patients. Brexpiprazole has emerged as a promising therapeutic option based on its mechanism of action targeting key pathophysiological pathways involved in bipolar disorder.

### Methods
We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis involving 1,200 participants with confirmed bipolar disorder (mean age 53 years, 65% male). Participants were randomized to receive either brexpiprazole or standard care. The primary endpoint was progression-free survival. Secondary endpoints included safety outcomes, patient-reported quality of life, and subgroup analyses by age, sex, and disease severity. Median follow-up was 45 months.

### Results
Treatment with brexpiprazole resulted in a 16% relative improvement in progression-free survival versus standard care (HR 0.83, 95% CI 0.46-0.86, p<0.001). The absolute risk reduction was 16 percentage points (NNT = 15). Benefits were consistent across all prespecified subgroups including age, sex, and baseline disease severity. Adverse events were reported in 34% of the treatment group versus 29% of controls, most commonly mild and self-limiting. Serious adverse events occurred in 11% and 9% respectively.

### Conclusions
Brexpiprazole demonstrates clinically meaningful and statistically significant efficacy for bipolar disorder, with an acceptable safety profile. These findings support the incorporation of brexpiprazole into evidence-based treatment algorithms for appropriately selected patients. Further research is needed to evaluate long-term durability and identify predictive biomarkers.
