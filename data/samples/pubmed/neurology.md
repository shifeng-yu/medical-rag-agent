## Dopamine Agonists for Migraine: A Prospective Cohort Study

**PMID:** 160000000 | **Year:** 2022

### Background
Migraine represents a significant cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, affecting millions of patients annually. Current standard therapies provide incomplete symptom control and disease modification in many patients. Dopamine Agonists has emerged as a promising therapeutic option based on its mechanism of action targeting key pathophysiological pathways involved in migraine.

### Methods
We conducted a prospective cohort study involving 2,500 participants with confirmed migraine (mean age 63 years, 55% male). Participants were randomized to receive either dopamine agonists or standard care. The primary endpoint was progression-free survival. Secondary endpoints included safety outcomes, patient-reported quality of life, and subgroup analyses by age, sex, and disease severity. Median follow-up was 18 months.

### Results
Treatment with dopamine agonists resulted in a 36% relative improvement in progression-free survival versus standard care (HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.56-0.96, p<0.001). The absolute risk reduction was 16 percentage points (NNT = 25). Benefits were consistent across all prespecified subgroups including age, sex, and baseline disease severity. Adverse events were reported in 27% of the treatment group versus 17% of controls, most commonly mild and self-limiting. Serious adverse events occurred in 12% and 10% respectively.

### Conclusions
Dopamine Agonists demonstrates clinically meaningful and statistically significant efficacy for migraine, with an acceptable safety profile. These findings support the incorporation of dopamine agonists into evidence-based treatment algorithms for appropriately selected patients. Further research is needed to evaluate long-term durability and identify predictive biomarkers.
