## ARBs for Ventricular Tachycardia: A Long-Term Follow-up

**PMID:** 160000000 | **Year:** 2023

### Background
Ventricular Tachycardia represents a significant cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide, affecting millions of patients annually. Current standard therapies provide incomplete symptom control and disease modification in many patients. ARBs has emerged as a promising therapeutic option based on its mechanism of action targeting key pathophysiological pathways involved in ventricular tachycardia.

### Methods
We conducted a long-term follow-up involving 1,200 participants with confirmed ventricular tachycardia (mean age 69 years, 58% male). Participants were randomized to receive either arbs or standard care. The primary endpoint was symptom severity. Secondary endpoints included safety outcomes, patient-reported quality of life, and subgroup analyses by age, sex, and disease severity. Median follow-up was 23 months.

### Results
Treatment with arbs resulted in a 17% relative improvement in symptom severity versus standard care (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.57-0.88, p<0.001). The absolute risk reduction was 34 percentage points (NNT = 25). Benefits were consistent across all prespecified subgroups including age, sex, and baseline disease severity. Adverse events were reported in 19% of the treatment group versus 12% of controls, most commonly mild and self-limiting. Serious adverse events occurred in 7% and 6% respectively.

### Conclusions
ARBs demonstrates clinically meaningful and statistically significant efficacy for ventricular tachycardia, with an acceptable safety profile. These findings support the incorporation of arbs into evidence-based treatment algorithms for appropriately selected patients. Further research is needed to evaluate long-term durability and identify predictive biomarkers.
